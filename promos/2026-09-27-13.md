**Title:** The Missing Layer in AI Tooling: Deciding What Your Agents Should NOT Do

**Tags:** ai, agents, productivity, workflow

**Body:**

We've gotten good at making AI agents *do* things. The hard part now is deciding what they shouldn't, and proving the rest is safe.

I run a small digital marketplace (iGen) for workflow-clarity products, and I test every tool against real agent workflows before we list it. Two gaps keep showing up in how people run agents today:

1. **No guardrails.** Most agent setups I see have zero permission boundaries. No blast-radius limits, no human-approval gates, no rollback plan for the day the agent decides to do something expensive. The horror stories on the front page this week (agents publishing hit pieces, agents bankrupting operators on DN42, agents deleting production databases) aren't exotic — they're what happens when autonomy outruns controls.

2. **No decision layer.** Everyone's learning to prompt better, but prompting is the wrong skill when the problem is *what to build*. If AI writes the code and AI reviews the code, the human's real job is deciding what deserves to exist at all. That's a framework problem, not a prompt problem.

So I shipped two small things to match:

- **Agent Guardrails Checklist** — a practical checklist: permission boundaries, blast-radius limits, human-approval gates, secret handling, rollback plans. Turn agent autonomy from a risk into a controlled process. $12.99
- **AI Build-Decision Template** — opportunity scoring, effort estimates, verification checklists, and what-not-to-build gates for choosing what AI should build next. $19.99

Both live on iGen.tech — a marketplace for exactly this kind of workflow-clarity product, no fluff, one-time prices, no subscriptions.

Not a pitch for a SaaS. Just two checklists that make the agent era boring in the good way.
